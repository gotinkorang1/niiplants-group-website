# 02 — Brand Guidelines

## Brand architecture: endorsed, not merged

Nii Plants Group uses an **endorsed brand model** (the same pattern Marriott
uses across its hotel brands, or Dangote across cement/sugar/foods): the
group identity provides trust and a visual "house style," while each
subsidiary keeps its own name and enough visual identity to feel like a
specialist in its own right.

In practice:

- The **group mark** (Nii Plants Group) anchors the main navigation, footer,
  legal pages, About/Governance, and Careers.
- Each **subsidiary** gets its own name, its own hero imagery, and — once
  branded assets exist — potentially its own accent color drawn from a
  constrained group palette (see [03-design-system.md](03-design-system.md)).
  It does not get its own typeface, logo lockup style, or layout system —
  those stay consistent so the site never feels like eight different
  vendors stitched together.
- Every subsidiary page carries a consistent "Part of Nii Plants Group"
  endorsement mark near the top, so a visitor arriving directly at a
  subsidiary via search still understands the parent relationship
  immediately.
- Five companies — Nii Plants and Car Rentals, Nii Plants Logistics, Trivoxo
  Limited Company, Puffs Ghana, and Plants Greene Leasing — already have
  live sites with their own visual choices (Plants Greene Leasing, for
  instance, already has real mission/vision copy and a distinctive stated
  core value around professionalism; Nii Plants and Car Rentals already has
  real testimonials and named staff). The group site's overview pages for
  these five (see [05-pages.md](05-pages.md)) should feel like the same
  family without contradicting what's already live — reuse their real
  facts/testimonials rather than inventing generic substitutes, per
  [04-content.md](04-content.md).

## Voice and tone

**Confident, understated, precise.** The group speaks the way an
established institution speaks: it states facts, does not oversell, and
never uses hype language.

Do:
- Lead with concrete capability ("A fleet of over X vehicles serviced
  across Accra and Kumasi") over abstract claims ("industry-leading
  solutions").
- Use active voice and short, declarative sentences.
- Let scale speak for itself — numbers, years of operation, geographic
  reach — rather than adjectives.
- Write each subsidiary's copy in a voice appropriate to its category
  (Hospitality can be warmer and more sensory; Logistics and Office
  Supplies should be plainer and more operational) while keeping grammar,
  formatting, and terminology consistent group-wide.

Avoid:
- Marketing filler: "world-class," "synergy," "cutting-edge," "one-stop
  shop," "unparalleled."
- Exclamation points, emoji, and casual address ("Hey there!").
- Vague superlatives that aren't backed by a fact on the same page.

## Messaging pillars

Every major page should reinforce at least one of these; the homepage
should touch all six across its sections:

1. **Trust** — governance, longevity, named leadership, client
   testimonials/logos.
2. **Scale** — breadth across eight sectors, fleet/property/facility
   counts, geographic coverage.
3. **Innovation** — how each subsidiary modernizes its category (digital
   booking, fleet telematics, modern logistics tracking) — shown through
   specifics, not the word "innovative."
4. **Professionalism** — consistent visual system, error-free multilingual
   copy if applicable, responsive contact channels.
5. **Luxury** — restrained, high-production photography and generous
   whitespace rather than ornamentation; luxury is communicated by what the
   design *doesn't* do (no clutter, no stock clip art) as much as by what
   it does.
6. **Long-term stability** — years in operation, repeat/institutional
   clients, consistent leadership, clear legal/registration information.

## Photography direction

- **Real, commissioned or licensed corporate photography only** — no
  generic stock-photo clichés (handshakes in front of glass buildings,
  isolated smiling call-center headsets, generic "diverse team laughing at
  laptop").
- Prefer environmental shots: actual vehicles, actual properties, actual
  operations, in real Ghanaian settings (Accra streetscapes, real fleet
  yards, real hotel interiors) over abstract or generic imagery.
- Consistent color grading across all subsidiary photography so the site
  feels like one system even though the eight sectors look visually
  different by nature.
- Every hero image should support the copy on top of it, not just fill
  space — see [06-components.md](06-components.md) for hero component
  rules on contrast and text legibility over photography.

## Typography direction (recommendation, pending brand assets)

- A **serif or high-contrast display face** for major headlines (H1/H2) to
  read as established and editorial rather than app-like — evokes Deloitte
  / Marriott-tier gravitas.
- A **clean grotesque/sans** for body copy, UI labels, and navigation —
  optimized for legibility at small sizes and across the multilingual
  content this group may eventually need (English + potentially local
  languages).
- No more than two typeface families total. No script/decorative fonts.
- Full scale defined in [03-design-system.md](03-design-system.md).

## Things to actively avoid

- Generic "multi-purpose business" template layouts (icon-in-a-circle
  three-column feature grids repeated on every page).
- Gradient-heavy SaaS aesthetics, floating 3D shapes, or illustrated blob
  backgrounds.
- Carousel/slider heroes that auto-rotate — prefer a single strong static
  or video hero per page.
- Overuse of iconography as a substitute for real photography or content.
